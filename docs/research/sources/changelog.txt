Faster Exports, Shareable Recipes, Photos to Video
Exports got faster and a lot more dependable this week. Recipes are easy to share, your photos can become clips, and the
Mac app runs lighter while the Director works.
Faster, steadier exports
Videos with motion graphics export up to 30% faster on Mac, so a minute-long edit that took 56 seconds now takes
about 40. Heavy 4K projects use far less memory along the way. Before an export says it's done, Cardboard now checks
that the file plays from start to finish, and makes it again if it doesn't.
Share a recipe
Found a recipe you love? Copy its link straight from the recipe and send it to a friend. If they're signed in, the link
opens ready to remix. Every recipe now takes any mix of video, photos and audio, and the dialog is simpler: one place
for your media, and Remix is always ready to go.
Turn your photos into clips
Start a generated clip from one of your photos, or use your pictures as references for a new still. When a clip needs an
opening frame, you approve it once and the frame and clip are made together. Pick the quality you want and see what it
costs before you generate, with a progress ring while it works.
A lighter Mac app
The Mac app uses about half the CPU while the Director is writing, and graphics with videos inside them keep up while
you scrub. You can also put your library anywhere: open Settings › Library and choose Change… to move it to another
folder or drive.

July 13, 2026#33
Keyframes, Blur, Familiar Shortcuts
The timeline just got a lot more capable. Keyframes live right on it now, clips snap and duplicate the way you expect,
blur joins your effects, and Cardboard can speak your editor's shortcuts.
Keyframes, right on the timeline
Open a track and its properties fan out into keyframe lanes. Drag the diamonds to retime a move, click one to jump there
and light up the matching field in the inspector, and press Delete to drop it. Collapse the clip and the keyframes ride
along as a marker strip. Press U to expand. Animate position, scale, opacity, blur, and more, with the preview following
your drag live.
Cut, duplicate, snap
The timeline keeps up with your hands now. Cmd/Ctrl+X cuts the selected clips, Alt/Option-drag drops a duplicate right
where you let go, and clips snap to each other's edges as you drag. Press S to toggle snapping off when you need to
place something by eye. Dragging several clips across tracks lands them cleanly.
Your shortcuts, your way
Press ? for the redesigned shortcuts dialog: a full keyboard you can hover to inspect and click to rebind,
with presets for Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, and CapCut. Bring the muscle memory you already have, or
build your own map. Your changes stick.
Gaussian blur
Blur is one of your effects now. Soften a background, ease a face out of focus, or keyframe it in and out over time.
